Making sense of a report

A test report should help you make a decision.

A report full of numbers is only useful if you understand what they mean for your farm. A soil test helps describe the soil sampled. A plant test measures the plant material collected. Water and produce tests answer different questions again.

The results need to be read alongside what is happening in the paddock or block. Your crop history, previous applications and observations help the agronomist work out what the results mean and whether a prescription blend or further testing is appropriate.

A result represents the sample taken.

Interpret the result alongside paddock or block details, crop stage and earlier tests before deciding what to do.

The test types

What does each test measure?

Choose a test for a specific purpose. The team can help confirm which sample is needed.

01

Soil

Helps assess the sampled soil, including pH, Carbon and Nutrient levels where selected. Keep the sampling depth and laboratory method with the result. A soil test does not show by itself what reached the plant.

Explore soil testing
02

Leaf and tissue

Shows what is present in the sampled plant material. Record crop stage, tissue choice, recent conditions and sampling method so the result can be interpreted.

Explore leaf and tissue testing
03

Differential sap

Uses separately labelled young and old tissue collected together. The paired results can be compared with each other and with repeat samples. A difference does not establish its cause or predict the crop’s response.

Explore differential sap analysis
04

Water

Assesses the quality of water used for sprays, fertigation or irrigation. Results need to be interpreted for the intended use.

Explore water testing
05

Produce

Measures the harvested crop. Composition, dry matter, yield and area help assess Nutrient removal. The amount removed at harvest is not automatically the amount of fertiliser needed next season.

Explore produce testing
Prescription blends

From your results to a blend for your farm.

The next step is to work out what your soil and crop need. This is rarely straightforward. The test results inform the recommendation, but your budget, application equipment and timing are part of the discussion.

01

Plan

Discuss the crop, area, what you want to achieve and the practical limits of the farm.

02

Analyse

Review the reports and sampling details you already have. Ask whether any further testing would help.

03

Prescribe

Work through the Nutrient requirements and a practical recommendation with the agronomist. Some soil requirements may need to be addressed over more than one season.

04

Supply

Discuss the prescription blend, application and supply arrangements with the team.
