Two ways to assess plant nutrition

Standard tissue testing and DSA. What is the difference?

Standard tissue testing measures Nutrient concentrations in dried plant material. DSA analyses extracted leaf fluid from young and old leaves separately, giving the agronomist another comparison to investigate.

Different methods. Different reference values.

Dry tissue and sap results are not directly interchangeable. Each needs its own sampling protocol, reporting units and crop reference. The team can help you choose the test that suits the question.

Why we use Differential Sap Analysis

There is more to compare than one leaf result.

A young-leaf result does not tell us what was measured in the older leaves. Keeping the two samples separate lets us compare their Nutrient levels and investigate the differences, rather than combine them into one result.

Follow the crop, not just a single number.

DSA is an important part of Hybrid-Ag’s prescription approach. The agronomist reviews each result and the difference between leaf ages with crop stage, load, soil and water information where available.

Comparable repeat pairs help us see what has changed since the previous test. A difference is a reason to investigate, not proof of its cause or an automatic instruction to apply a product.

Testing and liquid prescription blends

Use the result to guide the next prescription.

Testing is useful when it helps answer a practical question. We discuss what the crop needs, what the results support and whether more information is needed before changing the nutrition recommendation.

Where an application is needed, we discuss a custom Hybrid-Ag liquid prescription blend for the crop’s requirements and your equipment. Rate, timing and budget are part of that decision. A test result alone does not determine the blend.

Before sampling

A useful result starts with the right sample.

Tell us the crop and what you need to find out. We confirm the test and its current instructions before you collect the material.

01
Identify the crop and area

Keep crop, variety, paddock or block and growth stage with each result. Separate materially different areas.

02
Confirm which material to collect

Leaf ages, plant parts and sample amounts depend on the crop and test. DSA young and old leaves stay in separately labelled samples.

03
Follow the handling instructions

Dry tissue and sap tests can require different packaging and handling. Use the instructions supplied for the selected test.

04
Keep the sampling context

Record the date, stage, recent rain, irrigation and applications so the result can be interpreted and compared later.
