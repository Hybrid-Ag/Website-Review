Produce testing and interpretation

Produce testing and Nutrient removal.

Produce testing measures the composition of the harvested crop. Read alongside dry matter, yield and quality figures, it helps the team assess Nutrient removal and inform crop nutrition planning.

Interpreting the results

Read the sample alongside harvest records.

A laboratory concentration alone is not a removal figure or a fertiliser recommendation. Calculating removals also requires the harvested volume, units and relevant crop details.

01

Representative produce

The sample needs to represent the crop, variety and harvest unit being discussed rather than an isolated piece of produce.

02

Dry matter or moisture

Water content changes how concentration and harvested mass relate. The relevant basis needs to stay clear.

03

Yield and area

Harvest volume, units and the paddock or block area connect sample composition to the amount removed.

04

Nutrient removals

Calculated removals describe Nutrients exported with the harvested crop. They do not automatically equal the next input requirement.

05

Quality measures

Grade, pack-out, composition, maturity and processor records help relate the test results to the quality of the harvested crop.

06

Other test results and crop records

Soil, plant and water results, together with seasonal conditions and crop history, help explain the harvest results.

Across cropping systems

Match the interpretation to the crop.

The material sampled and the quality measures differ between crops. Comparisons need the correct crop details, units and production records.

Fruit and nuts
Connect composition and removal to yield, pack-out, grade, maturity and post-harvest condition.
Grain and oilseed
Connect harvested yield and composition to paddock history, quality specification and Nutrient export.
Vegetables and roots
Connect marketable harvest, grade, uniformity and crop-specific quality to the paddock or block.
Grapes
Connect fruit composition, yield and removals to the block, harvest timing and winery or market specification.
The Hybrid-Ag approach

Position. Pattern. Plan.

Harvest results are reviewed alongside the season that produced them and any further information needed for the next recommendation.

01Position

What left at harvest?

Establish the crop, sample, area, yield, composition and commercial measures for the paddock or block.

02Pattern

What helps explain the harvest results?

Review the result alongside soil, plant and water tests, crop stage, seasonal conditions, inputs and previous harvests.

03Plan

What changes next?

Use the results to guide the next review and identify any further testing or records needed.

Before sampling

Trace the sample back to the crop.

01
Crop and variety

Name the harvested crop, variety or relevant plant material and the production system.

02
Paddock or block

Record where the sample came from, the harvest date or stage and how the sample was selected.

03
Yield and units

Bring the harvested quantity, area and units so calculations are tied to the correct basis.

04
Harvest and market records

Include relevant grade, pack-out and processor, winery or market specifications.
