Grower perspective

Manna Farms

The family at Manna Farms describes why they approached Hybrid-Ag and how the working relationship developed.

Read transcript

[Music]

We're fourth-generation farmers from Mildura. My grandfather, great-grandfather and dad have all been involved in farming this way. Dad bought these farms in this region back in 1969, and started to grow his own business from there.

Around the late '80s, he was having some problems with soil compaction, and just a decline in the trees and so forth. He was pointed across to biodynamic farming by his sister, actually. The first farm was certified biodynamic in 1989, and since then it's been a lifestyle and a philosophy we've decided to run with.

It's now my brother Rob and myself who own and run the business. In 2009, I suppose we were probably somewhere around 550, 600 acres. Over the following three years, we expanded and expanded and expanded, and we've ended up now with 1,300 acres: just under 400 acres of citrus, around 500 acres of almonds and 15 acres of avocados. We've now got this big beast of a biodynamic farming operation.

We just want to produce the best-quality fruit we can, whether we're organic, conventional, biodynamic or whatever it may be. It's all about taste and flavour. Producing a really nice, nutrient-dense fruit probably relates to eating quality. The better the fruit tastes, that's going to bring return customers and obviously drive profitability in the business.

As the farming industry changes, people want to know this paddock-to-plate stuff. They want to get in touch and in contact with who it is that's growing their fruit, what their properties look like, and be actively involved or emotionally tied to the actual farm itself.

There's going to be more importance on regenerative farming, looking after the soil and being potentially carbon neutral. People will drive and push us as farmers to look after the land a lot better. Climate change is obviously a massive issue. Farming is probably one industry that can fix it pretty much straight away, if we really wanted to, across the world.

We originally brought them on board around 2011, I think it was. The main reason was a change in some of the composting we were using. We were putting out straight cow manure, and putting out quite a lot of it. We talked to the guys at Hybrid Ag about what they could do for us, and found that a few tweaks in the compost blend could significantly reduce the amount we were putting out. So we decided to give it a go, and we did see some great results straight up.

That relationship's only grown, especially over the last three or four years. Tom and I often sit down in the office and have a bit of a chinwag about life, philosophies about farming, and what we should be doing to look after the land and the soil, and leave it in a better place than we started. Having that trust with him and what he believes has given us the flexibility to let him take charge of some aspects and areas we're not happy with.

It's a nice, smooth, easy relationship. It's happy days from my point of view.

We've probably got an anniversary of 50 years next year of growing. We're second generation. We currently have a third generation on board, which is pretty encouraging.

I think generally farming is probably something that's in your blood. I always had a passion from a young age to grow stuff. I've done nothing else but grow fruit. I've lived where I currently live for about 49 or 50 years of my 52 years of life.

You get into a situation where it's not a forced situation. It's a good lifestyle. The only problem is the amount of hours you work a week. We do 60 hours as a quiet week, and 80's not uncommon.

Since Gavin and I have been in business together, which is 26, 27 years, we've probably achieved what two generations do in our time frame together. We've built a pretty big pack house. It's been sink or swim, and we've been swimming pretty hard.

Well, we used to grow a lot of cherries years ago, and through the association meetings, Tom was always there and in our ear: “Give me a go, give me a go.” It would be 14, 15, 16 years, something like that, since we first gave him a try on one block that was struggling a little bit. Now we use Hybrid Ag fertilisers across the whole farm.
