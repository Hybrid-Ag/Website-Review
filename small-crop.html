Small Crop

With a short growing cycle, there is less time to assess a problem and respond. We work with you to develop prescription blends for your crop, using test results and field observations to understand its requirements at each stage and the market it needs to supply.

Production priorities

Optimising inputs, maximising outcomes.

Saleable yield and the grade your customer needs come first when we plan your prescription nutrition. Your test results and growing conditions help us target what the crop needs.

01

Marketable yield

The saleable portion of the crop, not simply everything harvested from the paddock or block.

02

Grade and specification

The retailer, market or processor sets the grade the crop needs to meet.

03

Uniformity and timing

How evenly the crop develops and whether it will be ready for the planned harvest.

04

Quality and holding

Quality needs to hold through packing and storage, or meet the processor’s requirements.

Understanding the block

Recommendations need the block’s history.

The previous planting and conditions in the block help us understand the current crop. We use that information to plan a prescription that suits your production system, equipment and budget.

01

Production target

The crop’s destination, required grade and planned harvest help define the result the block needs to achieve.

02

Crop characteristics

Crop, variety, planting method and actual growth stage help the team interpret field observations and plant results.

03

Site and resources

Soil conditions, water source and quality, irrigation and application equipment inform what is practical during a short growing cycle.

04

Season and history

Previous crops and applications, test dates and harvest results help explain the current crop and what to consider before the next planting.

Crop families

Support for different crop types.

Testing and recommendations take account of how the crop grows, its current stage and what the customer requires.

Leaf

Leafy greens and herbs

Spinach, lettuce, rocket, mizuna, tatsoi, chard, kale, herbs and microgreens.

Head

Brassicas

Broccoli, cauliflower and cabbage, where formation, uniformity and harvest specification inform the recommendations.

Root

Roots and alliums

For carrots and onions, consider establishment and crop development through to harvest and the required market grade.

Tuber

Potatoes and tuber crops

Consider saleable size and quality alongside processor or market requirements.

Across the crop cycle

Plan around the crop’s growth stage.

The time from planting to harvest varies with the crop, season and production system. We base recommendations on how each planting is developing and what it needs to produce.

01

Before planting

Set the crop and market target, review rotation and previous results, and establish the soil and water context for the paddock or block.

02

Establishment

Check emergence or transplant establishment, root-zone conditions and how evenly the crop begins.

03

Vegetative growth

Assess canopy or leaf development and plant results against the crop’s current requirements.

04

Crop formation

Review leaf, head, root or tuber development against the crop’s intended use.

05

Finish and harvest

Review marketable yield, uniformity, quality and harvest timing against the required specification.

06

Review and reset

Review the harvest results and what changed during the season before planning the next crop.

Reviewing the crop

What can the last crop tell us?

We look back at the conditions at planting, what happened during growth and the harvest results. Together, these help explain the crop’s response and give us a starting point for the next prescription.

Starting conditions
Crop, variety, rotation, production system, soil, water and the intended destination.
Plant response
Stage, field observations, representative sampling and the timing behind each result.
Commercial outcome
Harvest, pack, market or processor information interpreted against the original target.
Next planting
What is worth repeating, what needs another comparison and what should change next planting.

The Hybrid-Ag approach

Position. Pattern. Plan.

We work through these questions with you before recommending the next application or planning for another crop. Your production system and the market you supply are part of that discussion.

01Position

Where is this crop now?

Establish the crop and variety, paddock or block, production system, stage, market target and the evidence available.

02Pattern

How did it get here?

Review the previous crop and rotation, test dates and applications alongside weather, field observations and harvest results.

03Plan

What should happen next?

Agree on what needs attention now, what needs checking and what to consider for the next planting.
