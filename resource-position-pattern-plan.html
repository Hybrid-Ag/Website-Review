Working through the results

What do the results mean for your farm?

A test result needs an explanation. Where was the sample taken? What was growing there? Have the results changed since the last test? The answers can affect what the agronomist recommends.

Hybrid-Ag uses Position, Pattern and Plan to work through that information with you. Your knowledge of the farm matters alongside the reports. Together they help the team assess Nutrient requirements and whether a prescription blend is appropriate.

The detail still matters.

These questions help organise the information. They do not replace the agronomist's judgement.

The three questions

Understand how the recommendation was reached.

Keep what the test measured separate from what may explain it. Then discuss the proposed action and any information still needed.

01

Position

Where are you now? Record the crop, variety, sampled area and growth stage alongside the available tests and field observations. These are the starting facts to review.

02

Pattern

How did you get here? Compare previous tests with seasonal conditions, input history, field observations and harvest results to understand what may have changed.

03

Plan

What should happen next? Discuss the proposed action and timing, any remaining uncertainty and whether further observations or testing are needed.

Across cropping systems

Adapt it to the growing system.

The questions are the same, but the crop cycle and production goals differ. Keep the paddock or block details with the results.

01Horticulture

The block

Consider the crop, variety or rootstock, growth stage and crop load alongside water and plant tests and fruit results.

02Small Crop

The paddock or block

Follow the fast crop cycle, rotation, marketable harvest, uniformity and customer specification.

03Broadacre

The paddock

Consider differences between paddock zones alongside rotation, establishment, seasonal conditions, yield and quality.

04Viticulture

The block

Consider variety and rootstock, canopy growth and crop load alongside fruit composition and the required harvest specification.

Working with the team

Advice you can understand and discuss.

A useful recommendation explains what the results show, why an action is being suggested and what is still uncertain. If more information is needed, the agronomist should explain why it would help.

A prescription also needs to suit the way you farm. Your budget, equipment and timing matter when deciding what can be done now and what may need to be addressed later.
