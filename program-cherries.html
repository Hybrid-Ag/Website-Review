Production priorities

Optimising inputs, maximising outcomes.

Fruit size and firmness come first when we plan your prescription nutrition. We use your block’s test results and orchard history to plan for the grade and pack-out you need.

01

Marketable yield

The total weight picked does not show how much fruit meets the buyer’s grade.

02

Size and uniformity

Fruit size and the spread of sizes across the block are assessed against the grade you are aiming for.

03

Firmness and condition

Fruit firmness and physical condition matter at harvest, through packing and on to sale.

04

Pack-out and shelf life

Pack-out shows how much fruit meets the required grade. Shelf life matters because the fruit must hold its condition after picking.

Understanding the block

Read the results with the block’s history.

Before preparing a prescription, we discuss your test results and the history of the block. We then work with you on what can be addressed with the equipment and budget available.

01

Market target

The market you supply determines the specification the fruit needs to meet. Size, grade and condition after picking help define the production target.

02

Variety and crop

We need to know the variety, actual growth stage and crop load, as well as the condition of the canopy.

03

Soil and water

Root-zone conditions and the water supply help explain the soil results. We need to know the water source and quality, and how the block is irrigated.

04

Season and history

Previous tests, applications and growing conditions help explain changes through the season. Harvest results and the condition of the trees after picking are useful when planning the next prescription.

Across the season

Nutrition through the cherry season.

The tree’s nutrition requirements change through the season. We consider its current stage of development together with what has happened earlier when reviewing the program.

01Post-harvest

Review the harvest.

The fruit and pack-out results help us assess the block’s performance. We also consider reserves and root-zone conditions before planning for next season.

02Senescence and dormancy

Assess reserves and tree condition.

As the trees move towards dormancy, the season’s history and the condition of the soil, canopy and trees help us assess reserves and plan the work ahead.

03Bud and flower

Assess early growth.

We look at early growth and flowering with the block’s test results, allowing for stored reserves, weather and the crop the trees may carry.

04Set and cell division

Follow crop load and development.

Fruit set and early development give us more information about the crop. We consider these together with the plant results at this stage.

05Expansion and ripening

Keep quality in view.

As fruit develops, we consider water supply, canopy condition, Nutrient uptake and crop load in relation to the market specification.

06Harvest and review

Measure what reached market.

Fruit condition, pack-out, grade and harvest results give us information to use when planning the next season.

Tests and block records

Testing needs interpretation.

We discuss the results with you alongside the history of the block and what has been observed in the orchard. This helps us work out what needs attention.

Soil and roots
Mineral balance, soil structure and root-zone conditions affecting Nutrient access.
Water
Quality and delivery across irrigation, fertigation and foliar work.
Leaf, tissue and sap
Plant test results considered alongside growth stage, crop growth and crop load.
Fruit and removals
Quality, pack-out, yield and what left the block with the harvested crop.
