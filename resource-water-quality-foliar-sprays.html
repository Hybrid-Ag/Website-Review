Resources Water quality and foliar sprays

Water is part of every foliar spray.

Before preparing a foliar prescription, check the water you will use. A water report helps the team assess it alongside the crop’s Nutrient requirements and current product directions.

Understand the measuresRequest water testing
Before mixing

Water quality can affect a spray mix.

Dissolved minerals, suspended material and treatment residues can affect the way a foliar mix behaves. The source water needs to be considered with the intended Hybrid-Ag products, whether they form a prescription blend or are applied separately.

Send the team the full water report, including the sample date and reporting units. Explain where the water comes from, any treatment it receives and which crop and application you are considering. A hardness result alone cannot establish whether a mix is suitable.

pH and alkalinity are not the same measurement.

pH shows how acidic or alkaline the water is when tested. Alkalinity measures its capacity to neutralise acid. Hardness mainly reflects dissolved Calcium and Magnesium.

Reading a water report

Know what each measure represents.

Interpret each measurement for the intended use. No single number gives a complete assessment of water quality.

01

pH

How acidic or alkaline the sample is when tested. The reading can change after treatment or mixing, but it does not tell you the water’s capacity to neutralise acid.

02

Alkalinity and bicarbonate

Alkalinity describes the water’s capacity to neutralise acid and resist pH change. Bicarbonate may also be reported separately, so check which result and reporting basis you are reading.

03

Total hardness

Commonly reflects dissolved Calcium and Magnesium. Ask for the reporting basis so one result can be compared with another.

04

EC and dissolved salts

Electrical conductivity indicates the combined contribution of dissolved salts. It does not identify the individual salts or elements behind the reading.

05

Specific constituents

Sodium, chloride, Boron, Iron, Manganese and other measured elements may matter differently for sprays, fertigation and irrigation.

Australian reporting units

Check the units before comparing results.

Overseas guidance may use grains per gallon, Fahrenheit or gallons per acre. Check the units and reporting basis before comparing those figures with an Australian laboratory report.

Checking treated water

Test the water after treatment.

Different treatments change different properties of the water. The treatment name alone does not tell you whether the water is suitable. Test it where it fills the spray tank or irrigation system.

Filtration

May remove suspended material without removing dissolved hardness or alkalinity. Check the system specification and the treated result.

Softening

Exchanges ions rather than simply removing everything dissolved. Review Sodium, chloride, hardness and the intended use.

Acidification

Changes pH and may neutralise part of the alkalinity. A lower pH reading does not prove that hardness or buffering has disappeared.

Reverse osmosis

Can reduce dissolved salts, but the output depends on feed water, membrane condition, recovery and maintenance. Verify the water produced.

Before the foliar mix

Use the report with the product directions.

Arrange the current sampling instructions with the team before collecting water. Identify the bore, dam, river, tank, mains supply or blend, and sample the water that actually fills the spray unit. Keep the source and treatment details with the report so a later result can be compared properly.

For a proposed foliar prescription, the team also needs to know the crop, intended products, equipment and application conditions. Mixing order, compatibility, pH adjustment and water-conditioning requirements must follow the current Australian label and product-specific technical advice.

Review whether another test is needed when the source or treatment changes, including seasonal changes to the supply. A previous report describes the sample tested, not every future tank fill.

A jar check is not proof of crop safety.

It does not prove product compatibility or show how the crop will respond. Follow the current label and the team’s technical advice.
